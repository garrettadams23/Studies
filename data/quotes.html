❝ Sourced Quotes — Why This Collection Has Citations QUOTES • Start Here ▶
Core Concept

An unsourced quote is a rumour with good grammar

Quote collections are the most misattribution-dense writing on the internet. A line gets attached to a famous name because it sounds like something they would say, the pairing gets repeated, and after enough repetitions nobody checks. Einstein, Mark Twain, Churchill and the Buddha attract these the way a magnet attracts filings — the more quotable someone is, the more they get credited with things they never said.

So every quote here carries the work it comes from, and where a popular line cannot be traced, this collection says so instead of quietly dropping it. That is the whole point of the last card.

"Do not seek to have events happen as you want them to, but instead want them to happen as they do happen, and your life will go well."Epictetus, Enchiridion 8
"The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way."Marcus Aurelius, Meditations 5.20 (Hays translation)
"You have power over your mind — not outside events. Realise this, and you will find strength."Marcus Aurelius, Meditations — a widely used modern rendering rather than a single fixed translation
The trap

"The obstacle is the way" is a title, not a sentence Marcus wrote

The five-word version is the title of Ryan Holiday's 2014 book. What Marcus actually wrote is Meditations 5.20, above — the same idea, in a longer and less slogan-shaped form. Nothing dishonest happened; a modern author distilled a passage and the distillation became famous. But quoting the short form as Marcus Aurelius credits him with a sentence he did not write.

This is the most common failure mode with translated ancient texts, and it is worth a rule: if a quote from a Greek or Roman source is unusually punchy in English, suspect a modern hand. Ancient prose translated faithfully rarely sounds like a motivational poster.

Concept

The truncation that reverses the meaning

The header of this site once carried "Do not seek for things to happen the way you want them to. — Epictetus". That is the first half of Enchiridion 8, and the half that carries the actual instruction is the second: "but instead want them to happen as they do happen." Cut in half, it reads as a warning against wanting things. In full, it is a specific technique — align your wanting with what is, rather than abandoning wanting.

Truncation is a quieter problem than misattribution and often does more damage, because the words really are the author's.

☯️ Eastern Quotes — Tao Te Ching, Zen & the Buddhist Canon QUOTES • Eastern ▶
Reference

Traceable to a work

QuoteSource
"The Tao that can be told is not the eternal Tao."Tao Te Ching, chapter 1 — the opening line, in one of many translations
"Nothing in the world is softer than water, yet nothing is better at overcoming the hard and strong."Tao Te Ching, chapter 78
"Before enlightenment, chop wood, carry water. After enlightenment, chop wood, carry water."A Zen proverb — correctly attributed to a tradition rather than a person, which is the honest form when no author is known
The trap

"The Buddha said" is the least reliable attribution in circulation

A very large share of lines circulated as the Buddha's cannot be located in the Pali Canon or any early source. "Peace comes from within. Do not seek it without" is one of them — widely repeated, warmly received, and without a traceable origin in the tradition it is credited to.

This is not an argument that the sentiment is wrong. It is an argument that "— Buddha" is doing work the sentence has not earned. Where a genuine canonical source exists it can be cited precisely, by nikāya and sutta, the same way Meditations is cited by book and section. When nobody can produce that citation, the honest label is "modern aphorism", not a name from 2,500 years ago.

The same caution applies to the Tao Te Ching for a different reason: it genuinely is ancient, but it has been translated dozens of times with very different results. Two people quoting "chapter 78" may be quoting sentences that share almost no words. Name the translation when it matters.

Reference

Traceable to a work

QuoteSource
"One must imagine Sisyphus happy."Albert Camus, The Myth of Sisyphus — the closing line of the essay
"If we have our own why in life, we shall get along with almost any how."Nietzsche, Twilight of the Idols, "Maxims and Arrows" 12. Usually met second-hand, because Viktor Frankl quotes it in Man's Search for Meaning
Amor fati — "love of fate"Nietzsche's own term, used in The Gay Science 276 and Ecce Homo. A concept he named rather than a quotation
"The unexamined life is not worth living."Plato, Apology 38a — Socrates speaking at his trial. Everything we have of Socrates comes through other writers; there is no text by him
Concept

Second-hand is not wrong, but it should be labelled

Two of the four above reach most readers through an intermediary — the Nietzsche line through Frankl, and everything Socratic through Plato. That is normal and unavoidable, and it is not a defect as long as the chain is stated. "Socrates, in Plato's Apology" is a more truthful citation than "— Socrates", and it costs three words.

🚫 Misattributed — Famous Lines Nobody Can Source QUOTES • Corrections ▶
Core Concept

Three of these were on this very site

The rotating quote in this page's header carried eighteen lines. Checking them for this card found that several of the most-repeated had no traceable source in the work of the person credited. They have been corrected, and they are listed here rather than quietly removed — a collection that hides its own errors teaches the wrong lesson.

Reference

Popular attributions that do not hold up

QuoteCredited toWhat is actually known
"Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication."Leonardo da VinciNot found in his notebooks. Its popularity traces to a 1977 Apple marketing brochure. Whoever wrote it, there is no evidence Leonardo did
"In the middle of difficulty lies opportunity."Albert EinsteinNo reliable source in his writings or recorded remarks. A perennial on Einstein quote lists, none of which cite anything
"To know yourself is the beginning of all wisdom."AristotleNot located in the Aristotelian corpus. It reads like a restatement of the Delphic maxim know thyself, which long predates Aristotle and belongs to no single author
"Peace comes from within. Do not seek it without."The BuddhaNo early canonical source. A modern aphorism in Buddhist clothing
"The obstacle is the way."Marcus AureliusThe title of a 2014 book by Ryan Holiday, distilled from Meditations 5.20. The idea is Marcus's; the sentence is not
The trap

"It's still a good quote" is true and beside the point

None of these lines become less useful for being unsourced — "in the middle of difficulty lies opportunity" is decent advice regardless of who said it. The problem is the borrowed authority. Attaching Einstein's name is a claim that one of history's great minds endorsed this, and that claim is false. Say the sentence without the name and it stands or falls on its own, which is where it belongs.

The habit generalises well beyond quotes, and it is the reason this card sits on a technical reference site: a claim's source is part of the claim. The same instinct that checks whether Aristotle really said something is the one that checks whether a Stack Overflow answer applies to your version, or whether a vendor benchmark measured what it says it measured.
